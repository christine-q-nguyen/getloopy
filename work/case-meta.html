07 · Job search in a box

Preparing for a job hunt is overwhelming. So I built the whole job search in a box, from thirteen months of evidence.

The portfolio was only one painful part. A job search also needs a résumé for each kind of role, a straight answer on which roles fit, and a way to keep up with a market that changes every week. I built all of it with agents, from my own records, and approved every claim before it went public.

The problem

AI roles get new names every few months: AI enablement lead, agentic product manager, technical deployment lead. I needed to know which ones fit my experience, and to prove it. But my work was scattered across chat threads, tickets, wiki pages, calendar invites, a Git history, and document libraries. I needed one story, a résumé for each kind of role, and a way to keep up.

My role

I was the operator and the reviewer. An agent drafted the plan and I edited it twice before anything ran. Agents did the digging; I approved every risky step and made the final call on every claim.

The decision

Let the evidence change the story. A claims ledger tagged every résumé claim verified, flagged, estimate, or retire, and the digging had to confirm, correct, or remove each one. I approved the risky writes and let the reads run, so the agents could work without getting the final word.

I went into recruiter and hiring-manager conversations prepared: which roles fit, which résumé to send, and what to say about the gaps.

How I knew it worked

Every number on this site had to earn its place. The ledger checked each claim against a system of record before it went public. One headline number didn't survive, and several got more specific.

Who owns it now

I do, and it runs every week. The method is open source with made-up data, so you can run your own. Mine stays private.

What I learned
  • Systems of record beat memory. I rediscovered work I'd forgotten about that made a real impact.
  • One résumé doesn't fit every role. Tailoring from the same checked claims kept every version honest.
  • It's never finished. This is a reflection of me and my story, so I keep iterating on it. Getting really loopy!
