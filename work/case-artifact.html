Turn one discovery meeting into requirements your developers can build from.

Record the meeting. Get back what a developer needs to start: the problem, the users, the data, what good looks like, and Jira-ready stories, each tied to what was said. It's the method that took my intake at work from four to six months to about a week, rebuilt from scratch so your team can use it.

The problem

Requirements from a discovery call usually take weeks. Someone re-listens, re-types, and re-argues what was said, and the developers still start from a blank page. I was sure the fix was to encode the discovery method itself: keep a person's decision inside the workflow, and write down what good looks like before anyone argues about a demo. Now you can try it. It's the workflow that saved my team hours and cut our timelines from months to about a week. Your developers start from a draft, and you find out early whether you can build it yourself.

My role

I designed and wrote it alone, from scratch: no employer code, prompts, data, or names. It's tested on three made-up discovery calls.

The system
  1. record the calla Zoom, Teams, or captions file
  2. pull out the requirementsthe problem, users, data, success criteria, and more, each tied to a quote
  3. score itis it wanted, buildable, and worth it, and what nobody said out loud
  4. three yeseswho was there, whether the summary is right, whether to write the stories
  5. stories outJira-ready, with acceptance criteria and the quotes behind them

It runs offline on a laptop. An optional MCP server lets an AI assistant read the results; it can't change anything.

How I knew it worked

It checks itself against answers a person wrote down, and it shows where it breaks. Below a confidence of 0.7, a field goes to a person instead of being accepted, because that's where mistakes start to climb.

Who owns it now

You can. It's free and MIT licensed. Point it at a recording of your own discovery meeting and see what your developers would get.

Repository: github.com/christine-q-nguyen/transcript-to-requirements. Its sister project: a job search in a box.

What I learned
  • Quotes build trust. Every field points to what was said, so the person approving can check it instead of taking it on faith.
  • Say where it's weak. People trust a tool more when it tells them where it breaks.
