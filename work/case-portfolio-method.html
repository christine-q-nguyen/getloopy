My job search in a box, rebuilt so you can run yours.

It starts from your records, not your memory. You get a story you can defend, a résumé for each kind of role, a straight read on which roles fit, and a weekly digest of new roles at the companies you care about. The demo runs on a made-up person, so you can see every step before you point it at your own work.

The problem

Most people write their portfolio and résumé from memory, then spend countless hours hunting for evidence, capturing visuals, and sanitizing work. Even then, it often doesn't fit the role or the market they're applying to. I started from the records instead, for my own job search.

My role

I designed and wrote all of it from scratch: no employer code, prompts, data, or names. The demo follows a made-up enablement lead, Aria Chen, at a made-up company, and every claim and number in it is invented.

The decision

Nothing goes public without your yes. Reading your records never waits. Marking a claim verified, or retiring it, does: you read the list, then approve it. The résumés, the fit check, and the digest only read what you approved.

The system
  1. write it downevery claim you want to make
  2. check itagainst tickets, chat, calendar, Git, documents, and meetings
  3. you approvenothing is public until you say yes
  4. tailor ita résumé for each kind of role, from approved claims only
  5. keep upwhat each role asks for, and a weekly digest ranked by fit

It runs on your laptop. The digest can read public job boards (Greenhouse, Ashby, Lever), and a read-only MCP server lets an AI assistant look at the ledger without approving anything.

How I knew it worked

The check comes with the answers a careful reviewer would give, and it prints where it disagrees: 6 of 7 claims right. The miss is the point. It's a true fact that lives in only one system, a monthly review on a calendar, so it asks a person instead of guessing.

$ python3 ledger.py eval
accuracy  6/7  (0.857)
weak spot C07: got flagged, gold verified
Who owns it now

You do. Swap in your own claims, exports from your own tools, the roles you're weighing, and the companies you're watching, then run the digest every Monday. My real run stays private.

Repository: github.com/christine-q-nguyen/portfolio-from-evidence. The story behind it: my job search in a box.

What I learned
  • Single-source facts still need a person. Don't turn 86% into a headline; publish the miss.
  • Approve the writes, let the reads run. You stay in charge without approving every step.
